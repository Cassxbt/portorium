Descriptive literature was provided for our review. The item under consideration is described as an optical radiation exposure chamber system, which is a custom-built, single-unit designed apparatus housed within a 20-foot shipping container. The system is specifically built to perform Light-Induced Degradation (LID) testing on photovoltaic (PV) modules by subjecting them to controlled optical radiation and temperature. This allows the system to simulate years of outdoor field exposure in a compressed timeframe to evaluate their reliability, durability, and long-term performance. Operationally, the apparatus features a Ceramic Metal Halide (CMH) and Quartz Tungsten Halogen (QTH) lamp array that emits optical radiation (spanning the ultraviolet, visible, and infrared spectrums) characterized against the AM1.5G reference spectrum. Irradiance is verified using a silicon reference cell, and the exposure temperature is actively maintained at 167 degrees Fahrenheit. The electrically powered system incorporates integrated temperature controllers, variable-speed fans, and a dedicated mini-split air conditioning unit to regulate the environment within the electronics and control chamber.
