The item under consideration is described as the Discal Dirtmag air and dirt separator (546-5461-NA546  Series), which is a magnetic air and dirt separator engineered for hydronic systems, integrating three crucial  functions: air separation, dirt separation, and ferrous impurity separation. This device continuously removes  entrained air, including micro-bubbles, from water or glycol-based fluids within closed-loop heating and  cooling systems. Micro-bubble formation, often occurring in components like boilers or under cavitation  conditions, is effectively addressed, with the separator removing air down to the micro-bubble level with  minimal head loss due to its specialized internal body design. Key components include a polypropylene air  vent float, peroxide-cured rubber seals, and stainless-steel linkages and guide pins, ensuring unimpeded float  movement and resistance to residue accumulation. The Discal Dirtmag operates by combining fluid velocity  reduction and coalescence. As fluid enters the larger separator body, its velocity decreases, facilitating the  release of dissolved air as micro-bubbles, consistent with Henry’s Law (lower pressure and higher  temperature promote gas release). The fluid then passes through an internal mesh element with concentric  surfaces, inducing a “whirling” motion that causes micro-bubbles to adhere. These adhered bubbles coalesce,  increasing in volume until their buoyancy overcomes the adhesion force, allowing them to rise into a large air  chamber at the top of the unit. A float-operated automatic air vent then releases the accumulated air to the  atmosphere while preventing system fluid escape. The device is designed for a maximum working pressure  of 150-200 psi and an operating temperature range of 32°F to 270°F.
