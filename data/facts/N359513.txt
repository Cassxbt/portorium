Descriptive literature was provided for our review. The item under consideration is described as The DISCAL air separator, model 551-5517-NA 551 Series,  which is a device engineered for the continuous removal of entrained air, including micro-bubbles, from  water or glycol-based fluids in closed-loop hydronic heating and cooling systems. Micro-bubble formation  frequently occurs in system components such as boilers and devices operating under cavitation conditions. This separator removes air down to the micro-bubble level with minimal head loss, attributed to its  specialized internal body design. Key components include a polypropylene air vent float, peroxide-cured  EPDM (Ethylene Propylene Diene Monomer rubber) seals, and stainless-steel linkages and guide pins,  ensuring unimpeded float movement and resistance to residue accumulation. The device operates by  combining fluid velocity reduction and coalescence. As fluid enters the larger separator body, its velocity  decreases, facilitating the release of dissolved air as micro-bubbles, consistent with Henry’s Law, which  states that lower pressure and higher temperature promote gas release from a liquid. The fluid then passes  through an internal mesh element with concentric surfaces, which induces a “whirling” motion, causing  micro-bubbles to adhere. These adhered bubbles coalesce, increasing in volume until their buoyancy  overcomes the adhesion force. The enlarged bubbles subsequently rise into a large air chamber at the top of  the unit. A float-operated automatic air vent then releases the accumulated air to the atmosphere while  preventing the escape of the system fluid. The device is designed for a maximum working pressure of  150-200 psi and an operating temperature range of 32°F to 270°F.
