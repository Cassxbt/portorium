Photographs and a sample  was submitted and sent to the Customs laboratory for analysis. The merchandise is a stainless-steel water bottle. You indicate that the item is a double-walled vacuum  drinking vessel. The stainless-steel water bottle measures approximately 25 centimeters high and has a  diameter of 7.2 centimeters. The water bottle has a white stainless-steel exterior with a flat bottom, a bamboo  screw-top cap, and a swiveling stainless steel handle connected to the top cap. On the exterior of the water  bottle, are printed with the words “National Geographic,” “Expeditions,” “Lindblad,” and the logo. The  drinking vessel is designed to keep beverages at a fairly constant temperature for reasonable periods of time. The volume capacity is 600 milliliters. Laboratory analysis of the water bottle indicates that the drinking vessel primarily consists of iron,  chromium, nickel, and manganese. The results indicates that the vessel is stainless steel. The cap is  comprised of a hollow stainless-steel body, a steel handle, and a wooden top. The wooden top appears to be  bamboo based on the color and grain pattern, and is 6.2% of the total weight of the vessel. The inner and outer walls of the vessel are two different stainless-steel variants. Laboratory analysis  determined that there is a vacuum between the sealed double walls of the outer wall of the tumbler. The outer  wall of the tumbler was breached to admit air into the space between the walls. After breaching the outer  wall, an inner wall was noted and the temperature rate of change of the hot water placed in the vessel  increased. The space between the two walls was not packed with insulating material, nor was there a  protective outer casing around the item to perform temperature retention.
