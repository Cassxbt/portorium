Descriptive literature was provided for our review. The item under consideration is described as the “Crawler” EM G3V2 motorized electromagnetic inspection robot (item number 1022756), which is a remote-controlled robotic platform designed for the non-destructive internal condition assessment of dewatered and depressurized water pipelines. The device features a modular, motorized track chassis that is lowered into the pipeline through access points such as manholes. It remains connected to a surface-based control console via an umbilical cable that facilitates the continuous transmission of electrical power, operator control commands, live high-definition video feeds, and diagnostic sensor data. The device does not perform any maintenance or repairs to the pipe. During operation, the Crawler is navigated by a technician who utilizes onboard high-definition cameras and LED lighting for real-time visual steering. The core diagnostic capability of the device is provided by an advanced electromagnetic sensor array. This array emits low-frequency electromagnetic fields into the pipe walls, which interact directly with metallic components, such as steel cylinders, prestressing wires in concrete pressure pipes, or ductile iron. By measuring distortions in the returning electromagnetic signatures, the sensors detect and quantify critical structural anomalies, including wall thinning, corrosion, and broken structural wires. This data is mapped against an internal odometer, allowing engineers to pinpoint the exact location and severity of distress to facilitate targeted repairs.
