The merchandise under consideration is a dedicated physical hardware server designed for centralized SIM card management, identified as the IQ-SRX-20, SimrackX, SRX640, and Simrack640. All four model designations represent one single, identical physical product (SRX server). The model name variations exist solely for branding, marketing, and commercial channel differentiation. There are no physical, electrical, hardware, or mechanical differences among these four variants in their condition as imported. The SRX server functions as a dedicated physical hardware server designed for centralized SIM card management. The server is used in telecommunication networks to store up to 640 physical SIM cards (organized into 20 removable SIM trays) centrally. The device allows SIM card resources to be allocated over an IP network to distant, VSIM-enabled mobile devices on demand. The primary use is for mobile network testing. The manufacturing process for the SRX consists of sourcing components from Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Taiwan and China to a facility in France. There, Surface Mount Technology (SMT) is used to populate the printed circuit boards (PCB), creating one Mainboard printed circuit board assembly (PCBA), five Backbone/Backplane PCBAs, and twenty Tray PCBAs (SIM holders). The completed PCBAs are placed into a metal enclosure where they are integrated with a Raspberry Pi CM5, and plastic subassemblies inside the enclosure. Firmware flashing, operating system installation, and system configuration, as well as
functional testing and quality control take place next. Finally, the teh SRX is packaged alongside a 60W Mean Well power supply unit, a C13 power cable, and an ethernet cable before being exported to the United States for retail sale.
