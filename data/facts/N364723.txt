In lieu of a sample, photographs showing the front and back of representative finished articles were submitted with your request. The subject merchandise is identified as decorative textile wall hangings in various designs, shapes and sizes, typically ranging from 5 to 18 inches. The items are produced by inserting acrylic, cotton, and wool yarns into pre-existing cotton woven fabric using an electric tufting machine. The tufting process creates a loop-pile surface. The pile surface is secured with adhesive on the reverse side of the cotton cloth, and a secondary backing of felt is applied. The felt fabric is fitted with a metal D-ring hanging fixture so that the finished article may be displayed on a wall. You state the textile fiber composition of the pile surface varies by design; however, in all cases, the surface area consists chiefly of (more than 50 percent) acrylic yarn with lesser quantities of cotton and/or wool yarns, depending on the piece. You also state that the wool yarn is a product of Canada, but the acrylic and cotton yarns, woven cotton fabric, and felt backing may be sourced from various suppliers and may include materials manufactured outside Canada, the United States, or Mexico. For purposes of this ruling, we assume all materials other than the wool yarn is of foreign origin. Tufting and final assembly of the finished article are performed in Canada.
