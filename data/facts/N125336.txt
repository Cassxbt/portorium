The VVT is incorporated into an automobile’s engine for the purpose of varying the timing of the intake valves by adjusting the relationship between the camshaft drive chain and the intake camshaft. The VVT is a 2-stage hydraulically controlled cam phasing system. You state in your request that the part is made entirely of steel. The VVT is comprised of the following parts:housingrotoron and off chamberThe VVT is mounted to the front of the camshaft; oil pressure allows the VVT to adjust the position of the camshaft in the following manner. Once the oil enters either the right or left side of the oil control valve (“OCV”) it then flows through the camshaft to the VVT. When the VVT’s chambers are filled with oil, it causes the rotor to move which then turns (or changes the position of) the camshaft. As the camshaft rotates, the VVT instantaneously speeds up or slows down the phase of the camshaft (i.e., advances or retards the intake camshaft timing), adjusting when the valves open to allow clean air to enter or dirty air escape. These adjustments in the time between the exhaust valve closing and the intake valve opening results in improved engine efficiency, which is critical for fuel economy and emissions.
