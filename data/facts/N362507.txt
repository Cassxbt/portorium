Descriptive literature was provided for our review. The item under consideration is the Fournier Rotary Press, which is a dewatering system for industrial and  municipal sludge consisting of a sludge pump, a polymer tank, a polymer metering pump, a flocculator and a  rotary press connected through a series of pipes, hoses, and electrical wiring. The sludge pump feeds sludge  into the flocculator where it is combined with a pre-set ratio of polymer solution that enters the flocculator  inlet by means of the polymer metering pump. Through a short period of agitation, the flocculator transforms  the sludge and polymer solution into good quality “floc” (flocculated sludge), which is then directed to the  rotary press. The rotary press is a circular channel delimited by parallel, rotating chrome-plated stainless steel  filter elements on both sides. As sludge travels through the channel, filtrate (free water) is removed from the  sludge by the filter elements and flows to a collection area at either side of the press where it is routed to a  common outlet at the bottom of the unit. Due to the moisture being extracted, the sludge increases in density  as it travels through the channel, being pushed along by the friction of the filter elements. A restrictor bar  also exerts back pressure on the sludge by means of an air bellow system to keep the sludge from passing  through the channel too quickly. The pressure the sludge exerts on itself as it becomes progressively more  condensed, combined with the back pressure of the restrictor, ultimately forms the sludge into a dry, solid  cake that is extruded through the channel outlet.
