The first item under consideration is described as the PowerBox CAN-ASA, which is an electronic air-data instrument designed for aircraft applications, including unmanned aircraft systems (UAS). The device is equipped with two high-precision digital barometric pressure sensors used to measure static and dynamic pressure. It electronically processes static pressure to determine the aircraft’s altitude and rate of climb, while air speed is calculated from the differential between the static and dynamic pressure. The device supports an airspeed measurement range exceeding 850 km/h and delivers altitude measurements with a relative resolution of 100 mm. For data transmission and external connectivity, the unit incorporates an integrated CAN-BUS interface to communicate with flight computers, and it is capable of operating P2-BUS and EX-BUS telemetry interfaces in parallel. Physical connection to the aircraft’s Pitot-static pressure system is facilitated via two integrated 3 mm pneumatic connectors. The PowerBox CAN-ASA acts as a processing instrument that supplies calculated, real-time aircraft air-data information to external flight systems but does not independently navigate or control the aircraft. The second item under consideration is described as the Pitot-HASA (Heated Airspeed Altitude), which is an integrated electronic air-data instrument designed for aircraft applications, including unmanned aircraft systems. Structurally, the sensor probe incorporates two pressure sensors, a ceramic heating element, an integrated temperature sensor, a microcontroller, and associated electronic circuitry. Functionally, the integrated microcontroller processes the raw sensor measurements and outputs comprehensive aircraft air-data, such as RawAir, True Airspeed (TAS), and Indicated Airspeed (IAS), transmitted through a DroneCAN interface. To prevent icing and ensure accurate readings, the Pitot head features an actively regulated heating system capable of maintaining an adjustable operating temperature between approximately
30°C and 50°C. Configuration and firmware updates for the device can be executed via the CAN bus or a PowerBox Mobile Terminal. The Pitot-HASA functions as a data-gathering instrument that supplies measured and processed atmospheric flight data to an external flight computer but does not independently navigate or control the aircraft. The PowerBox CAN-ASA incorporates a bare printed circuit board (PCB) and housing components of Chinese origin. Additional electronic and mechanical components are sourced from several countries, including Germany, Japan, the United Kingdom, and the United States. The Pitot-HASA incorporates a bare printed circuit board and several mechanical and electromechanical components of Chinese origin. Additional electronic components are sourced from several countries, including Japan, Taiwan, Germany, and the United States. The printed circuit boards used for the PowerBox CAN-ASA and Pitot-HASA are supplied to Germany as bare, unpopulated and non-functional PCBs. PCB population and electronic assembly are performed in Germany using surface-mount technology (SMT), through-hole technology (THT), with specialized electronic manufacturing equipment, and qualified personnel. Following PCB population and inspection, the completed PCB assemblies remain in Germany for the subsequent product-specific manufacturing operations. These operations include firmware programming, mechanical and electrical final assembly, product-specific calibration, functional testing, final quality control and final product approval. All remaining electronic, mechanical and electromechanical components are combined with the populated PCB assemblies in Germany to create the finished PowerBox CAN-ASA and Pitot-HASA products.
