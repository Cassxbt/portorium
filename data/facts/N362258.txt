Descriptive literature was provided for our review. The merchandise under consideration is the IHT Spirit Heart Rate Monitor (Spirit HRM), a wrist-worn,  battery-powered, Bluetooth-enabled wireless wearable health device engineered to monitor and track  physical effort. This device features a secure, multi-component plastic housing comprising a top cover, a  bottom cover, a sealing rubber gasket, and an adjustable wrist strap, all designed within an IP-rated  waterproof enclosure for active use. Internally, its operation is managed by a Main Board containing a  Microcontroller Unit (MCU), control circuitry, a rechargeable internal battery, and a digital display screen. Magnetic charger connectors are integrated into the lower casing for recharging. The system incorporates an optical heart rate sensing module (OHR Board) that utilizes  photoplethysmography (PPG) technology. It emits light through a dedicated OHR lens via LED emitters into  the user’s skin, while photodiode sensors capture backscattered light signals to measure volumetric blood  changes. The device calculates and displays real-time physiological and operational metrics, including heart  rate (beats per minute), heart rate zone status, minimum/maximum heart rate, elapsed time in active zones,  activity goal progress, date/time, and battery depletion status. Wireless data transmission is facilitated by an  integrated Bluetooth module. System activation, long-term historical data logging, reporting, and group  metric management require pairing with a compatible host smart device running the proprietary Spirit  Software Suite, which includes classroom, assessment, and individual platforms. The unit does not support  cellular or standard smartphone notifications such as calls, emails, or text alerts.
